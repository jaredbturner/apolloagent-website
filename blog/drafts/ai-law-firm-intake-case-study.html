Most law firms have an intake problem they don't fully see. New client calls come in throughout the day — and often the evening and weekend. A receptionist answers some of them. Others go to voicemail. A paralegal follows up when they have time. By the time someone actually talks to the prospect, it's often been 24 hours or more.

According to ClaireAI's 2026 Legal Intake Benchmark — the largest-ever study of law firm intake performance, covering 1,000 U.S. firms across 50 states — the average law firm misses 35% of inbound calls, and the average annual revenue loss from missed and mishandled intake is $250,000 per firm. For personal injury firms, where case values are highest, that number climbs to $410,000 per year. The cause isn't negligence. It's a structural problem: intake is a high-volume, time-sensitive workflow being handled by people who have other jobs to do.

This is what AI agents are genuinely good at fixing. Here's how one mid-size firm did it — and what happened to their numbers when they did.

The $250K Problem: Why Legal Intake Is Broken

Before getting into the case study, it's worth understanding the economics. The same ClaireAI benchmark found that 61% of inbound calls to law firms happen outside business hours — evenings, weekends, holidays, and before 9 AM. And when a prospect calls, time is everything: response within five minutes drives a 400% conversion lift versus calling back the next business day (independently corroborated by Forrester and InsideSales research).

The math is brutal. If your firm gets 100 inbound inquiries per month and misses 35% of them, that's 35 potential clients going to voicemail — or, more likely, calling the next firm on Google. At an average case value of $4,200 and a 14% first-contact conversion rate, that's roughly $20,000 in lost revenue per month you never see because no one answered the phone fast enough.

The solution isn't to hire more receptionists. It's to stop treating intake like a human-only problem.

The Firm: A 14-Attorney Practice Hitting a Growth Ceiling

The firm in this case study — a 14-attorney practice with offices in two states, handling family law, criminal defense, and general civil matters — had been growing steadily for three years. But in early 2025, they hit a ceiling. New client numbers had plateaued despite an increase in marketing spend. Their intake coordinator was handling calls, web forms, referrals, and consultation scheduling simultaneously — and doing all of it manually.

When they ran the numbers, the picture came into focus: their front-desk team was answering roughly 68% of inbound calls during business hours. After hours and on weekends, that number dropped to near zero. They were spending approximately 12 hours per week per staff member on intake-related tasks — call qualification, scheduling, data entry into their case management system, sending retainer agreements, and following up on unsigned documents.

None of those 12 hours per week were billable. None were strategic. All of them were things a well-designed AI system could handle.

The Specific Pain Points

The firm mapped their intake process in detail before building anything. This step — which they spent two weeks on — turned out to be the most valuable part of the entire project. Here's what they found:

  • After-hours call handling: Zero coverage between 5 PM and 9 AM, and on weekends. Prospects who called during these windows left voicemail or hung up.
  • Lead qualification lag: After a call was answered, it took an average of 2.3 days to get a qualified prospect booked for a consultation. Most of that delay was back-and-forth scheduling and the time required to pull conflict checks.
  • Manual CRM entry: Every intake interaction required manual data entry into Clio — name, contact info, matter type, referral source, notes. The intake coordinator was spending 90 minutes per day on this alone.
  • Retainer follow-up: Unsigned retainer agreements sat unaddressed for days. The firm had no automated follow-up sequence — someone had to remember to call or email, and they often didn't until a week had passed.
  • Inconsistent qualification: Different staff members asked different questions during intake. There was no consistent rubric for identifying high-value vs. low-value cases, so time was wasted on inquiries that were never going to become clients.

The AI Agent Stack They Built

The firm implemented a four-component AI intake system over about six weeks, using a combination of purpose-built legal AI tools and a workflow automation layer built in Make.com to connect everything together.

Component 1: AI Voice Receptionist

An AI voice agent handles all inbound calls 24/7. When a prospect calls, the agent answers within one to two seconds, introduces itself, and conducts a structured intake interview: name, contact information, matter type, opposing parties (for conflict screening), how they heard about the firm, and a brief description of their legal issue. The agent is calibrated to the firm's specific practice areas — so it asks the right qualification questions for a family law matter versus a criminal defense inquiry.

The agent can handle multiple calls simultaneously — something no receptionist can do — and doesn't have good days and bad days. It asks the same questions in the same order every time, using the same empathetic tone the firm designed. Calls that require urgent legal attention (custody emergencies, arraignment deadlines) are escalated immediately via text to the on-call attorney.

Component 2: Automated Conflict Check and Case Scoring

The moment a call ends, the intake data flows automatically into Clio — the firm's case management platform — and a conflict check runs against existing client and matter records. In most cases, this takes under 30 seconds. If a conflict is found, the intake coordinator is notified automatically; if no conflict exists, the lead is scored based on the firm's qualification rubric (matter type, jurisdiction, complexity, case value estimate) and assigned a priority tier.

Clio Work's agentic capabilities — launched earlier this year — handle much of this orchestration. Attorneys can now issue goal-based prompts like "review this intake and identify any red flags before the consultation" and Clio's AI executes the full multi-step review autonomously, surfacing relevant precedent and flagging potential issues before the attorney has read a single word.

Component 3: Automated Consultation Scheduling

High-priority leads receive an automated text message within minutes of their call: a personalized message from the firm (sent via the intake system, not an obviously robotic number) with a direct booking link for a 30-minute consultation. The link connects to a real-time calendar showing attorney availability. No phone tag. No back-and-forth emails. Most consultations are booked within 20 minutes of the initial call.

Component 4: Retainer Automation and Follow-Up

After a consultation converts, the retainer agreement is generated automatically from a pre-approved template, populated with the client's details, and sent via DocuSign within minutes of the attorney marking the matter as retained. A four-step automated follow-up sequence begins immediately: a reminder at 24 hours, another at 48 hours with a direct signing link, a phone call trigger at 72 hours (routed to the intake coordinator), and a final check at day 7. The firm's retainer turnaround time dropped from an average of 9 days to under 48 hours.

How the System Handles a Real Intake Interaction

It's worth walking through a concrete example to make this tangible. A prospect calls at 9:40 PM on a Thursday — well outside business hours, historically a dead zone for this firm.

  1. The AI voice agent answers in under two seconds. It introduces itself by name, mentions it's with the firm, and begins the intake interview.
  2. The prospect describes a pending custody matter. The agent asks the relevant qualification questions: children's ages, current custody arrangement, jurisdiction, whether litigation has already begun.
  3. The call ends. Data is pushed to Clio automatically. Conflict check runs: no conflict found. The matter is scored as a high-priority family law lead.
  4. The prospect receives a text within four minutes: "Hi [Name], thanks for calling [Firm]. We'd love to schedule a 30-minute consultation with one of our family law attorneys. Here's a link to book a time that works for you: [link]."
  5. The prospect books a consultation for the following Tuesday morning at 10 AM. A calendar invite is created for both the prospect and the assigned attorney. The case file in Clio is automatically populated with the intake notes and a summary of the matter.
  6. The consultation happens. The attorney marks the matter as retained. Retainer agreement is generated and sent via DocuSign within minutes. Signed within 18 hours.

The intake coordinator's involvement in this entire flow: zero, until the consultation prep brief lands in their inbox the morning before the meeting. What previously required multiple phone calls, manual scheduling, CRM entry, and chasing a retainer signature happened automatically, overnight, without anyone touching it.

Results: Six Months In

The firm measured outcomes across six months post-implementation. The numbers were significant enough that they shared them publicly (with identifying details anonymized) as a benchmark for other firms evaluating similar investments.

  • After-hours call coverage: 0% → 100%. Every call is now answered, at any hour, on any day. This alone recovered an estimated $180,000 in previously lost annual revenue.
  • Intake staff time on manual tasks: down 78%. The intake coordinator now handles escalations, complex situations, and relationship-building — not data entry and scheduling logistics.
  • Lead-to-consultation booking time: 2.3 days → 22 minutes. The fastest part of the funnel is now the part that used to be the slowest.
  • Retainer turnaround: 9 days → 47 hours. Fewer lost clients at the post-consultation stage due to retainer follow-up delays.
  • New client volume: up 34% year-over-year — without a corresponding increase in marketing spend. The same number of inbound inquiries, captured and converted at a higher rate.
  • Client satisfaction scores: unchanged. The firm's Google Reviews held steady at 4.8 stars, with no complaints about AI-handled intake. Several positive reviews specifically mentioned how quickly the firm responded to their initial call.

The total implementation cost, including tooling, setup, and the initial six weeks of configuration and testing, was approximately $18,000. The revenue increase in the first six months alone exceeded $200,000. That's not a technology investment — that's a business decision.

What They'd Do Differently

The firm's managing partner was candid about what they'd change if starting over:

"We spent too much time trying to perfect the AI voice agent before we launched it. We should have launched with a functional version and refined it based on real calls. The first two weeks of live calls taught us more than the four weeks of internal testing."

A few other hard-won lessons worth passing on:

  • Map the process before touching the technology. The two weeks they spent documenting every step of their existing intake process — in detail, including the edge cases and exceptions — was the highest-leverage work they did. You can't automate a process you haven't defined.
  • Don't hide that it's AI. The firm was transparent that their initial contact system was AI-assisted. Prospects didn't care. What they cared about was being heard quickly and being able to book a consultation easily.
  • Integrate with your case management system first. The highest-friction part of their old process was manual CRM entry. Solving that one bottleneck — automatically pushing intake data into Clio — delivered immediate, measurable time savings before any of the fancier automation was in place.
  • Build an escalation path before you go live. The AI agent handles 80–85% of intake interactions fully autonomously. The remaining 15–20% — complex situations, distressed callers, unusual matter types — need a clear path to a human. Define that path before you launch, not after.

How to Start Building This at Your Firm

You don't need to replicate this firm's full stack on day one. Here's a practical sequence for firms that want to move in this direction without overbuilding:

  1. Start with after-hours coverage. Deploy an AI voice agent for after-hours calls first. This is the highest-ROI move with the least disruption — it doesn't change anything about how your team handles calls during business hours, but it captures everything you're currently missing at night and on weekends.
  2. Automate CRM entry. Get your intake data flowing directly into your case management system — whether that's Clio, Filevine, MyCase, or another platform. The time your team spends on manual data entry is not small. Eliminating it is fast, impactful, and often the first thing that makes your staff appreciate the system.
  3. Add retainer follow-up automation. Build a simple automated sequence for retainer signing. This is often easier to implement than it sounds — most document signing platforms have native automation features — and the impact on conversion is immediate.
  4. Measure before and after. Track your call answer rate, lead-to-consultation booking time, retainer turnaround, and new client conversion rate before you start. Check them again at 90 days. The numbers will tell you exactly where to invest next.
